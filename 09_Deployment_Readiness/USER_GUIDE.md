# User Guide

## Semiconductor Bank Credit Decision-Support Framework

---

## 1. Purpose

This guide explains how a credit analyst or reviewer should interpret the outputs of the semiconductor credit-risk framework.

---

## 2. Typical Workflow

Recommended workflow:

1. Select a semiconductor project
2. Review project characteristics
3. Review borrower credit-strength evidence
4. Review project stress vulnerability
5. Review Monte Carlo tail-risk indicators
6. Review portfolio concentration
7. Review external credit evidence
8. Review indicative model grade
9. Review credit posture
10. Review exposure posture
11. Review monitoring priority
12. Escalate to credit committee when necessary

---

## 3. Indicative Grades

### Grade A

Lower relative model vulnerability.

Typical interpretation:

- lower relative stress sensitivity,
- lower concentration impact,
- relatively stronger available evidence.

### Grade B

Moderate-low relative vulnerability.

Usually suitable for standard credit review, subject to complete underwriting.

### Grade C

Moderate vulnerability.

Typically requires enhanced review or additional information.

### Grade D

Elevated vulnerability.

Requires strong risk mitigants and enhanced monitoring.

### Grade E

Higher relative vulnerability.

Should generally be escalated for senior credit review.

---

## 4. Early-Warning Status

### GREEN

Standard monitoring.

### AMBER

Enhanced monitoring recommended.

### RED

Priority review required.

A RED status is not equivalent to default.

It indicates elevated model risk or significant stress sensitivity requiring closer assessment.

---

## 5. Borrower Credit Strength

Possible outputs:

- STRONG
- ADEQUATE
- WEAK
- VERY_WEAK
- NOT_OBSERVABLE

`NOT_OBSERVABLE` means sufficient verified borrower financial evidence was not available.

It should not be interpreted as poor credit quality.

---

## 6. Project Stress Vulnerability

The project stress score evaluates relative sensitivity to adverse conditions.

Possible classes:

- LOW
- MODERATE
- ELEVATED
- HIGH

This is not a probability of default.

---

## 7. Portfolio Concentration

The portfolio-concentration layer assesses whether financing the project could create disproportionate exposure through:

- project size,
- geographic concentration,
- allocation ranking,
- relative portfolio impact.

---

## 8. Credit Posture

Possible outputs may include:

- Proceed to standard credit review
- Proceed with enhanced credit review
- Enhanced due diligence required
- Proceed only with strong risk mitigants
- Defer or escalate for senior credit review

These are model-supported review postures, not automatic lending decisions.

---

## 9. Exposure Posture

Typical outputs include:

- Standard portfolio limits
- Moderate exposure with limits
- Limit and diversify exposure

Exposure recommendations should always be interpreted alongside the bank's own concentration limits and credit policy.

---

## 10. Important Analyst Checks

Before relying on the output, confirm:

- borrower identity,
- project identity,
- financial-year consistency,
- source reliability,
- project stage,
- rating date,
- stress assumptions,
- evidence completeness.

---

## 11. Human Decision Requirement

The final decision remains with the credit analyst and authorized credit committee.

The system should be used as additional analytical evidence, not as a substitute for professional judgement.

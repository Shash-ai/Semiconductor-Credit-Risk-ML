# Bank Pilot Proposal

## Semiconductor Credit Risk Decision-Support Framework

---

## 1. Pilot Objective

The purpose of the pilot is to evaluate whether the semiconductor credit-risk framework can provide useful supplementary decision support to bank credit analysts assessing semiconductor-related exposures.

The pilot is intended for validation and feedback only.

It is not intended for live automated lending decisions.

---

## 2. Pilot Scope

Recommended initial pilot scope:

- 5-15 semiconductor or electronics-related exposures,
- anonymized or non-production borrower data where possible,
- limited user group,
- credit-risk / corporate-banking analysts,
- controlled evaluation environment.

---

## 3. Pilot Questions

The pilot should evaluate:

1. Are the model outputs understandable?
2. Are the risk drivers relevant to analysts?
3. Does stress testing add useful information?
4. Is the borrower/project distinction meaningful?
5. Are portfolio-concentration signals useful?
6. Does the early-warning framework support monitoring?
7. Are indicative grades directionally reasonable?
8. What additional bank data would materially improve performance?

---

## 4. Proposed Pilot Workflow

### Step 1

Bank provides anonymized project and borrower inputs.

### Step 2

Data is mapped to the research framework.

### Step 3

The system generates:

- structural profile,
- stress vulnerability,
- tail-risk profile,
- borrower-strength evidence,
- concentration indicators,
- indicative grade,
- credit posture,
- monitoring status.

### Step 4

Bank analysts independently assess the same exposures using normal procedures.

### Step 5

Framework outputs are compared with analyst judgement.

### Step 6

Analysts provide structured feedback.

---

## 5. Pilot Evaluation Metrics

Suggested evaluation dimensions:

- interpretability,
- banking relevance,
- directional agreement with analyst judgement,
- usefulness of stress scenarios,
- portfolio-risk usefulness,
- workflow usability,
- false-alarm frequency,
- information completeness,
- model transparency.

---

## 6. Required Bank Data for a Stronger Pilot

Useful additional inputs would include:

- borrower financial statements,
- facility type,
- sanctioned exposure,
- outstanding exposure,
- interest rate,
- repayment schedule,
- collateral/security,
- guarantee information,
- internal credit grade,
- account conduct,
- days past due,
- restructuring history,
- covenant compliance,
- project implementation status.

These inputs should only be used under appropriate confidentiality and security arrangements.

---

## 7. Data Privacy

The research team should preferably receive:

- anonymized borrower identifiers,
- minimum necessary data,
- no personally identifiable customer information.

Sensitive data should not be stored in public repositories.

---

## 8. Pilot Success Criteria

A pilot may be considered successful if:

- analysts find the outputs understandable,
- model signals are directionally useful,
- stress-testing adds value,
- concentration analysis supports portfolio discussion,
- the tool improves consistency or speed of preliminary credit review,
- major methodological flaws are not identified.

---

## 9. Pilot Limitations

The current prototype:

- is not calibrated to observed default probabilities,
- is not a regulatory model,
- does not calculate expected loss,
- does not replace existing bank credit policy,
- requires human oversight.

---

## 10. Pilot Outcome

The primary outcome should be a structured validation report containing:

- analyst feedback,
- model strengths,
- model weaknesses,
- missing variables,
- recommended methodological changes,
- deployment recommendations.

---

## 11. Long-Term Vision

If sufficient real historical bank-credit data becomes available, the framework could later be extended toward:

- Probability of Default modelling,
- rating migration analysis,
- LGD/EAD estimation,
- expected-loss modelling,
- real-time early-warning systems,
- portfolio-level sector stress testing.

The current framework should therefore be viewed as a foundation for a future sector-specific bank credit-risk platform.
